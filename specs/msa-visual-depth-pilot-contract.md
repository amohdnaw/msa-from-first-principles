# Outcome contract — MSA visual-depth pilot

Agreed 2026-09-01. This contract governs the visual-depth pilot on MSA Level 4,
`%GRR, ndc, and against what`. The sibling contract is
`~/portfolio/specs/spc-visual-depth-pilot-contract.md`.

The pilot tests one production pattern before it is considered for the other
seventeen levels across both curricula. The inherited system in `DESIGN.md` still
governs the page.

For this visual-depth pilot only, this contract supersedes prior no-human-voice
clauses after both pilot levels are locked. Synthetic narration is the iteration
voice. Ammar's recording is the final locked cut.

## What you will see

1. Open `level-04.html`. The chapter remains readable from start to finish without
   completing a challenge, signing in, or watching a video.
2. Before Act A explains either ratio, the page asks the learner to predict whether an
   unchanged gauge can receive opposite verdicts. The prediction does not affect access
   or a score.
3. Act A builds one persistent measurement world. Gauge noise stays fixed while the
   part spread and specification boundaries move around it. The learner sees that the
   denominator is the question before either percentage appears.
4. Act A shows why improving the process can make `%GRR_study` worse while
   `%GRR_tolerance` does not move. The same gauge remains visibly unchanged through the
   transformation.
5. Act B moves that same gauge between two masked factories. One factory needs to sort
   close process populations. The other needs to decide conformance against a drawing.
6. Act B produces opposite verdicts from the same gauge. The learner sees why both
   verdicts are honest before the percentages name them.
7. Act B connects `ndc` to `%GRR_study` as the same information rearranged. The printed
   gates appear on one computed curve rather than as two rules to memorise.
8. The old Level 4 act is replaced by Act A. The page does not retain a redundant cut
   that teaches the same argument twice.
9. The two acts normally total 12–18 minutes. Runtime is not a gate. A shot stays only
   when it creates, transforms, tests, or applies the visual model.
10. After Act B, one optional challenge presents unseen data and a different surface
    story. The learner selects `Study variation` or `Tolerance`, chooses `use`,
    `improve`, or `replace`, then identifies the deciding field: `Decision job`,
    `Chosen denominator`, `Computed %GRR`, or `AIAG band`.
11. The pass condition maps `sort process populations` to `Study variation` and
    `judge drawing conformance` to `Tolerance`, reads the matching `msalab` ratio at
    printed precision, then maps `≤10 %` to `use`, `>10–30 %` to `improve`, and
    `>30 %` to `replace`. `Improve` is a next action, not a pass/fail verdict; the
    conditional tile remains neutral. A wrong answer names the mismatched field with
    the same label, shows both computed ratios and the applicable band, and retries
    with new data. The page never responds with only a red verdict.
12. Passing saves Level 4 progress in the browser. If storage is unavailable, the
    challenge still works for the current visit and says that progress was not saved.
13. The level exposes three secondary paths without replacing the chapter:
    `Use this when…` gives the field answer, `Evidence` shows the functions, seed, and
    tests, and a contextual link opens the matching MSA platform study.
14. That Level 4 contextual study is the one sitewide MSA platform link required by the
    parent contract. The current index navigation link moves here rather than creating
    a second link. A check counts exactly one MSA platform link across the site.
15. If either video is unavailable, the prose and transcript still carry the full
    derivation. The page does not mark the act watched.
16. Captions, transcript, and static keyframes preserve the argument when the learner
    reduces motion.
17. The finished acts have 1080p60 video, decoded audio bytes, non-empty WebVTT cues, a
    scored poster, and faststart playback.
18. With audio muted, a reviewer can state why the same gauge receives opposite verdicts.
    If narration owns that causal link, the storyboard fails.
19. No symbol appears before its quantity exists as a distance, spread, repeated
    measurement, or physical boundary.
20. Video, prose, challenge, and tests read the same `msalab` functions or compare
    against them at printed precision. No result is typed into more than one layer.
21. One novice explains why the denominator changes the question. One practitioner
    chooses the plant action without coaching. Both must succeed before this pattern is
    proposed for another level.

## Production sequence

1. Write one visual claim for the act.
2. Approve a shot-level storyboard that names the persistent object, surprise,
   transformation, counterexample, and decision.
3. Compute geometry and readouts from tested `msalab` functions.
4. Build the Manim cut with synthetic narration driving timing.
5. Build the unseen-data challenge and its exact feedback.
6. Watch every numerical, media, progress, and failure-state gate fail on a sabotaged
   copy before trusting it.
7. Record Ammar's voice only after the storyboard, visuals, script, and browser
   experience are locked.

## Failure behaviour

- A calculation failure preserves the learner's answer, names the failure, and never
  guesses a verdict or marks completion.
- A storage failure keeps the challenge usable for the current visit and never shows a
  false saved state.
- A video failure leaves the written derivation and transcript usable and never shows a
  false watched state.
- A wrong answer names the mismatched `Decision job`, `Chosen denominator`,
  `Computed %GRR`, or `AIAG band`, shows the computed ratios, and retries with new data.

## Not in scope

- Rebuilding any MSA level other than Level 4.
- Rebuilding any SPC act under this contract.
- Accounts, certificates, public rankings, or cross-device progress.
- Sitewide search or a full reference-mode redesign.
- New design tokens or a change to `DESIGN.md`.
- Publishing human narration before both pilot levels are locked.
- Real company data or identifiers.

## Defaults taken

- Reading stays open. Mastery is optional.
- Progress stays in the browser. There is no login or platform sync.
- Synthetic voice remains the iteration tool. Ammar's voice is the final locked cut.
- The masked case uses realistic semiconductor or manufacturing decisions.
- The pilot scales only after the SPC and MSA levels pass the same visual, numerical,
  browser, and human-use gates.
- After the pilot, each remaining level receives one verdict: keep its current act,
  rewrite one act, or earn a selective Act B. Two acts are never automatic.
